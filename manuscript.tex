\begin{abstract}
This document outlines the mathematical framework and empirical validation for the Cycle-Aware Forman-Ricci Curvature ($F^*$) applied to signed and bipartite networks. We resolve the metric collapse of standard Forman-Ricci Curvature in networks lacking 3-cliques by incorporating higher-order cycles ($C_4$) and structural balance weighting.
\end{abstract}

\section{Mathematical Formulation}

\begin{definition}[Cycle-Aware Forman-Ricci Curvature for Signed Bipartite Networks]
\label{def:curvature}
Let $G = (V, E, W)$ be a simple signed bipartite graph with symmetric adjacency matrix $A$ where $A_{uv} \in \{-1, 0, 1\}$. For an edge $e = (u, v) \in E$, let $d(u)$ and $d(v)$ denote the unweighted degrees of vertices $u$ and $v$. Let $\mathcal{C}_4(e)$ denote the set of all 4-cycles containing the edge $e$. Utilizing a strict penalty framework for structurally unbalanced cycles, the weight of a cycle $C \in \mathcal{C}_4(e)$ is determined by the algebraic product of its edge weights, $\text{sgn}(C) = \prod_{e' \in C} A_{e'}$. This yields a positive contribution ($+1$) for balanced cycles and a strict negative penalty ($-1$) for unbalanced cycles. The Cycle-Aware Forman-Ricci Curvature $F^*(e)$ is defined as:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:fstar}
F^*(e) = 4 - d(u) - d(v) + \sum_{C \in \mathcal{C}_4(e)} \prod_{e' \in C} A_{e'}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Non-Overcounting of 4-Cycles]
\label{lem:nonovercounting}
Let $e = (u,v) \in E$ be an edge in a simple signed bipartite graph $G$. The net 4-cycle weight summation $\Sigma_{C_4}(u,v) = \sum_{C \in \mathcal{C}_4(e)} \prod_{e' \in C} A_{e'}$ evaluates each 4-cycle containing $e$ exactly once without multiplicity, and is mathematically equivalent to the sparse matrix relation:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sigma_c4}
\Sigma_{C_4}(u,v) = A_{uv} (A^3)_{uv} - d(u) - d(v) + 1
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The matrix entry $(A^3)_{uv} = \sum_{x, y \in V} A_{uy} A_{yx} A_{xv}$ enumerates the sum of edge weight products for all walks of length 3 between $u$ and $v$. In a simple bipartite graph, any walk of length 3 from $u$ to $v$ is either a non-degenerate simple path $(u, y, x, v)$ with $y \neq v$ and $x \neq u$, or a degenerate walk that backtracks. 

The degenerate walks take three explicit forms:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item $u \to v \to x \to v$ for $x \in N(v) \setminus \{u\}$. There are $d(v) - 1$ such walks, contributing $A_{uv} A_{vx}^2 = A_{uv}$.
    \item $u \to y \to u \to v$ for $y \in N(u) \setminus \{v\}$. There are $d(u) - 1$ such walks, contributing $A_{uy}^2 A_{uv} = A_{uv}$.
    \item $u \to v \to u \to v$. This unique walk contributes $A_{uv}^3 = A_{uv}$.
\end{enumerate}

Summing the degenerate contributions yields $(d(u) - 1 + d(v) - 1 + 1)A_{uv} = (d(u) + d(v) - 1)A_{uv}$.

Any non-degenerate simple path $(u, y, x, v)$ corresponds to exactly one unique 4-cycle $C \in \mathcal{C}_4(e)$ closed by the edge $(u,v)$. The weight contribution of this path to $(A^3)_{uv}$ is $A_{uy}A_{yx}A_{xv}$. Multiplying this term by the incident edge weight $A_{uv}$ yields the full 4-cycle sign $A_{uv}A_{uy}A_{yx}A_{xv} = \prod_{e' \in C} A_{e'}$.

By isolating the simple paths and multiplying the expansion of $(A^3)_{uv}$ by $A_{uv}$ (noting $A_{uv}^2 = 1$), we obtain:
\begin{equation}
A_{uv} (A^3)_{uv} = \Sigma_{C_4}(u,v) + d(u) + d(v) - 1
\end{equation}
Rearranging this algebraic identity yields \cref{eq:sigma_c4}. This proves that evaluating the sparse matrix formulation extracts the exact net signed contribution of all 4-cycles overlapping at $e$ exactly once without duplication.
\end{proof}

\section{Empirical Validation}
The algorithm was validated on a structurally balanced signed bipartite network consisting of two partitions $U$ and $V$, where nodes were further divided into two cohesive structural groups $X$ and $Y$. Intra-group edges are strictly positive ($w = +1$) and inter-group edges are strictly negative ($w = -1$).
The exact structural topology and the corresponding evaluated Cycle-Aware Forman-Ricci Curvature ($F^*$) for each edge (formulated in \cref{def:curvature} and derived in \cref{lem:nonovercounting}) are illustrated in \cref{fig:graph}. As mathematically expected, all structurally balanced 4-cycles actively increased the curvature, successfully distinguishing densely cohesive subsets in the network.
